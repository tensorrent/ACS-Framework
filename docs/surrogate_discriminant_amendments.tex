\begin{abstract}
A \emph{marginal-matched surrogate discriminant} asks, of each statistic in a battery,
whether its value survives replacing the object by one drawn from the object's own
one-point distribution with all higher structure destroyed. Survivors are said to read
the distribution class (\emph{form}); collapses to read the object (\emph{function}).
The instrument is a specialisation of surrogate-data testing \cite{theiler} and was
introduced to decide a question that arises whenever many statistics agree: whether
their agreement is over-determination or redundancy. We observe that the operative
definition omits three elementary conditions, state each as a proposition, and measure
its consequences. \textbf{(1)} A witness invariant under the surrogate group has a
\emph{degenerate} null distribution --- a point mass --- so its standardised
significance is $0/0$; a small reported value is floating-point summation order
presented as evidence. \textbf{(2)} A witness with bounded range has a \emph{reachable
ceiling} on significance; where that ceiling falls below the decision threshold, no
object can be convicted and a verdict of \emph{form} is unearned. \textbf{(3)}
Non-invariance is necessary but not sufficient for a collapse, so \emph{form} is a
property of the pair (witness, object) and never of the witness. Recast, \emph{form}
is the statement that a witness \emph{factors through the marginal}: a descent
condition rather than a statistical one, which is why (1) is a corollary and not a
finding. We implement a four-tier verdict and measure the amendments on two corpora
sharing no subject matter: the first $2003$ non-trivial zeros of $\zeta$, where three
of six verdicts change and the original form/function split otherwise reproduces; and
a $6\times6$ $SU(2)$ lattice gauge configuration, where a battery of six witnesses is
shown to carry at most three independent readings, two of them exactly invariant and
two without power. We further localise a $0.4$--$1.0\sigma$ residual that FF06e
attributes to re-interpolation: it requires piecewise-linear interpolation
specifically and vanishes under exact inversion of the smooth counting function, so it
is removable rather than a caveat to be carried. None of the three propositions is new
as statistics; the contribution is that an instrument in use omits them, together with
the measured size of each omission.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A spectrum, a configuration, or a corpus admits no shortage of statistics. When many of
them behave as expected, the usual reading is over-determination: independent witnesses,
all agreeing, so the picture is robust. The opposite reading is available on the same
evidence. If the statistics are not independent --- if they are several lenses on one
underlying response --- their agreement is redundancy, and counting them as separate
confirmations is a category error. Worse, if what they share is generic to the
distribution class rather than specific to the object, their convergence reports only
that the object belongs to that class, which is true of uncountably many objects with
nothing else in common.

FF06e makes the question decidable with one operator. Replace the object by a
\emph{marginal-matched surrogate} --- same one-point distribution, higher correlations
destroyed --- and ask of each witness whether its value survives. A survivor was reading
the class (\emph{form}); a collapse was reading the object (\emph{function}), and the
size of the collapse measures its influence. There is no parameter to tune, because the
surrogate inherits the object's own distribution and the comparison is internal.

This is a specialisation of surrogate-data testing \cite{theiler}, whose structure is the
same: specify a null, generate ensembles consistent with it, and compute a discriminating
statistic on the original and on each surrogate. The specialisation is sharp and worth
stating, because the null here is not a model fitted to the data but the data's own
marginal, so the method is a permutation test with the object's empirical distribution as
the null.

Our concern is the \emph{definition} of the verdict, not the operator. We give three
conditions it omits, each elementary, each with a measured consequence.

\section{The discriminant}\label{sec:disc}

\begin{definition}[Surrogate ensemble]
Let $X$ be the object, decomposed into components $x_1,\dots,x_n$ carrying a
distribution. Let $G = S_n$ act by permuting components. A \emph{surrogate} is $gX$ for
$g$ drawn uniformly from $G$. By construction every surrogate has the identical
one-point distribution and no higher-order structure of $X$'s.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Witness, significance]
A \emph{witness} is a map $W\colon X \mapsto \mathbb{R}$. With surrogate mean
$\overline{W}$ and standard deviation $\sigma_W$ over an ensemble of $K$ draws, the
\emph{standardised significance} is
\[
  z(W) \;=\; \frac{\bigl|\,W(X)-\overline{W}\,\bigr|}{\sigma_W}.
\]
FF06e's Definition 2 declares $W$ \emph{form} when $z(W)$ is small ($\lesssim 3$) and
\emph{function} when it is large.
\end{definition}

The instrument is strictly stronger than the control most evidence disciplines require,
which is a null baseline measured on unrelated material. Unrelated material differs from
the object in \emph{both} its marginal and its structure, so a statistic that quiets down
on it may be responding to either. Holding the marginal fixed leaves only structure.

\section{Form as a descent condition}\label{sec:descent}

The statistical framing above obscures what is actually being asked. Let
$\pi\colon X \mapsto X/G$ send an object to its orbit --- equivalently, to its multiset
of components, which is exactly its marginal.

\begin{definition}[Factoring through the marginal]
$W$ \emph{factors through the marginal} if there is $\widetilde{W}$ with
$W = \widetilde{W}\circ\pi$, equivalently $W(gX)=W(X)$ for all $g\in G$.
\end{definition}

A form witness, in the limiting case, is precisely one that factors: its value is
determined by the marginal and the fibre over that marginal carries no information it can
see. A function witness does not factor; it sees the fibre. This is a local-to-global
statement --- the marginal is the quotient datum, and what the surrogate destroys is the
fibre --- and reading it that way makes the first amendment a one-line corollary rather
than an empirical discovery.

\section{Three omissions}\label{sec:props}

\begin{proposition}[Degenerate null]\label{prop:degenerate}
If $W$ factors through the marginal, the null distribution of $W$ is the point mass at
$W(X)$. Hence $\overline{W}=W(X)$, $\sigma_W=0$, and $z(W)=0/0$ is undefined.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$W(gX)=W(X)$ for every $g\in G$, so the pushforward of any measure on $G$ is
$\delta_{W(X)}$.
\end{proof}

The proposition is trivial; the consequence is not. FF06e's Definition 2 assigns
\emph{form} on the strength of a small $z$, and its headline table reports three
witnesses at $0.4$--$1.0\sigma$, while a construction remark states that the bare figure
for those witnesses is ``exactly zero by construction (symmetric-multiset invariants).''
Both cannot be read at once, and the reported figure invites being read as a small real
effect, which the remark denies.

A $0/0$ evaluated in floating point returns whatever the summation order leaves behind.
Measured on a lattice (\S\ref{sec:lattice}), where two witnesses are provably invariant:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Witness & $\sigma_W$ & computed $z$ \\
\midrule
mean $\operatorname{Re}U$ over links & $6.5\times10^{-16}$ & $\mathbf{1.4}$ \\
$\max\bigl|\,\|U\|-1\,\bigr|$ & exactly $0$ & $0.0$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A $z$ of $1.4$ arising from summation order alone falls inside the band FF06e reports,
on an object unrelated to the zeros and with no interpolation step present.

\begin{proposition}[Reachable ceiling]\label{prop:ceiling}
Let $W$ take values in $[a,b]$ and let the null distribution have mean $\mu$ and
$\sigma_W>0$. Then for every object $X'$ with that null,
\[
  z(W) \;\le\; C(W) \;:=\; \frac{\max(b-\mu,\;\mu-a)}{\sigma_W}.
\]
If $C(W)<\tau$ for decision threshold $\tau$, no object attains $z\ge\tau$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$|W(X')-\mu|\le\max(b-\mu,\mu-a)$ since $W(X')\in[a,b]$.
\end{proof}

Where $C(W)<\tau$, a low $z$ is a statement about the instrument's resolution and carries
no information about the object; declaring \emph{form} attributes to the object what
belongs to the test. Measured: a Wilson loop trace on a $6\times6$ lattice, with range
$[-1,1]$, has $\sigma_W$ between $0.11$ and $0.45$, giving $C$ below $5$ and in places
below $2$ against $\tau=3$. A synthetic case isolates the mechanism: a witness returning
one component of a sequence spanning $[-1,1]$ has $\sigma_W$ equal to the component
spread ($\approx0.58$) and $C\approx1.7$, so it can never be convicted however structured
the object.

FF06e's amendment of 24 September 2026 asks for exactly this condition --- ``a null
result must retain power and resolution limits'' --- but Definition 2 does not encode it,
so a reader following the definition cannot apply the amendment.

\begin{proposition}[Non-invariance is not sufficient]\label{prop:necessary}
Non-invariance of $W$ is necessary for $\sigma_W>0$ but does not imply $W(X)$ is
separated from the null. There exist non-invariant $W$ and objects $X$ with
$z(W)$ arbitrarily small.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By example. Let $W$ count ascents, $W(x)=\#\{i: x_{i+1}>x_i\}$, which is not
$G$-invariant. For $X$ a discretised sine period of length $n$, $W(X)$ is
$\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ or thereabouts, since a sine ascends on exactly half its period;
the null mean is $(n-1)/2$. Measured at $n=60$: $W(X)\in\{29,30\}$, null mean $29.5$,
$z=0.34$. The same $W$ on a monotone sequence of the same length gives $W=n-1$ and large
$z$.
\end{proof}

Hence \emph{form} is a property of the pair (witness, object). The practical consequence
is a reading of FF06e's own results: its distributional witnesses are form \emph{on the
first $10^5$ zeros}, which is what was measured, and not form as a standing property ---
they are serviceable statistics on an object whose spacing distribution differs from its
own shuffle.

\begin{remark}
None of the three is novel as statistics. Permutation tests require a statistic that
varies under the null, and any test requires power; both are standard. The contribution
is that an instrument in use omits them, and the measured size of each omission.
\end{remark}

\section{A four-tier verdict}\label{sec:tiers}

The propositions give a decision order, applied per witness, with $\varepsilon$ a
floating-point floor scaled to $|W(X)|$:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item $\max_i|W_i-W(X)|\le\varepsilon$ over the ensemble $\Rightarrow$ \tier{form\_exact};
      report no $z$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:degenerate}).
\item $\sigma_W\le\varepsilon$ but $W(X)$ differs $\Rightarrow$ \tier{function}, $z$
      unbounded. Every surrogate agrees with every other and not with the object.
\item $W$ bounded and $C(W)<\tau$ $\Rightarrow$ \tier{no\_power}
      (Prop.~\ref{prop:ceiling}). Unresolved, not form.
\item Otherwise \tier{function} if $z\ge\tau$, else \tier{form} --- a verdict about this
      pair (Prop.~\ref{prop:necessary}).
\end{enumerate}

A separate check detects a different redundancy: witnesses pairwise affine across three
or more objects are one witness in two costumes, and counting both double-counts.

\section{Calibration is a precondition}\label{sec:calib}

FF06e calibrates its engine against known invariants and known refractions with negative
controls and decoys, ``so that the engine could not become its own refraction.'' We find
this cannot be relaxed to good practice, because one failure mode has no other detector.

The caller must supply the surrogate construction, since only the caller knows what the
object's marginal is; this is the sole irreducibly domain-specific decision in the method.
If the supplied construction does not preserve the marginal, it is randomisation, and then
every witness reads \tier{function} for a reason having nothing to do with the object.
Measured: replacing the permutation with fresh random values convicts the sample variance
--- a provable invariant --- as \tier{function}. No internal consistency check catches
this and nothing about the output looks wrong. The only detector is the requirement that a
known invariant return \tier{form\_exact}.

The protocol, as implemented:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=1pt]
\item Known invariants $\Rightarrow$ \tier{form\_exact}, $z$ withheld.
\item Known non-invariant witnesses on a structured object $\Rightarrow$ \tier{function}.
\item A decoy returning noise independent of the object $\Rightarrow$ not \tier{function}.
\item A deliberately marginal-breaking surrogate $\Rightarrow$ must convict an invariant.
\item Fewer than two surrogates $\Rightarrow$ refuse to run.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Corpus I: the zeros of $\zeta$}\label{sec:zeros}

\paragraph{Instrument.} Riemann--Siegel $Z$ with two remainder terms \cite{edwards}, zeros
by sign change and bisection, $2003$ zeros on $t\in(20,2520)$, unfolded by the
Riemann--von Mangoldt term. Anchored before use: $\theta$ at the first Gram point
($17.8455995408$ against $17.8455995405$); the first ten zeros against published values,
worst deviation $2.5\times10^{-3}$; and the counts $N(50)=10$, $N(100)=29$, $N(300)=138$,
$N(1000)=649$, all exact, with $S(T)=N(T)-\langle N(T)\rangle$ bounded at
$+0.58,-0.00,+0.29,+0.38$. Positions carry $2.5\times10^{-3}$ --- the remainder is
asymptotic and $t\approx14$ is where it is weakest --- while the counts are exact. The
distinction matters: a missed zero is invisible locally and would appear only as $S(T)$
drifting.

\paragraph{Result.} Surrogate by permuting unfolded spacings; $K=40$; the arithmetic
witness is the prime-resonance power $\sum_{p\le29}\langle\cos(\gamma\log p)\rangle^2$ on
actual heights, as in FF06e.

\begin{tabular}{lrrrl}
\toprule
Witness & $\sigma_W$ & $z$ & $C(W)$ & Tier \\
\midrule

lag-1 spacing correlation & $2.16\times10^{-2}$ & $\mathbf{17.6}$ & $46.6$ & \tier{function} \\

FF06e's split reproduces: the arithmetic witness carries the overwhelming majority of the
object-specific influence and the distributional witnesses carry none.
Prop.~\ref{prop:degenerate} changes the last three verdicts from \emph{form at
$0.4$--$1.0\sigma$} to \emph{exactly invariant, significance withheld}. An amendment
altering no verdict would be a stylistic preference.

\paragraph{A check on amplification.} FF06e's decisive argument is that the function
signal sharpens with $N$ rather than averaging down, reporting $z_{\mathrm{arith}}=3030$
at $N=2\times10^4$ and $11\,497$ at $N=10^5$. The exact two-point exponent is
$0.829$, which extrapolated a decade below the fitted range predicts $450$. At our seed
we read $499.8$, but that is one draw of a $40$-surrogate estimate: over $300$ surrogate
seeds it spans $331$--$580$ ($95\%$), and with $3000$ surrogates it converges to $419.5$
($95\%$: $406.5$--$433.2$), $7\%$ below the prediction. Treating FF06e's two values as the
$60$-surrogate estimates they are widens the prediction itself to $228$--$847$ ($95\%$).
(Earlier versions of this paragraph said $4\%$, from an exponent rounded to $0.8$, and then
$11\%$, from the single draw. Both flattered or overstated a comparison the data cannot
make.) The growth with $N$ is not in question; the exponent is not testable at this
precision. Its paired caveat holds in the stated direction: the raw value is
\emph{larger} at smaller $N$ ($0.1762$ here against $0.0922$ and $0.0636$), so
significance and magnitude move oppositely. That the signal grows by more than an order of
magnitude across the three sizes is the evidence that the amplification is structure being
resolved; the third point's agreement with the fitted exponent is not, at this precision.

\section{Corpus II: lattice $SU(2)$}\label{sec:lattice}

A second corpus is needed because the amendments are claims about a method, and a method
validated on one object is accommodated to it.

\paragraph{Object.} A $6\times6$ periodic lattice carrying a unit quaternion
$U\in SU(2)$ on each link --- a Wilson lattice gauge configuration \cite{wilson}. The
marginal is the multiset of link rotors; the surrogate permutes which site each link
occupies. Spatial adjacency is what gauge structure consists of, a plaquette being the
ordered product around a face, so adjacency is the right structure to destroy.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Witness & smooth configuration & relaxed two-phase & note \\
\midrule
mean $\operatorname{Re}U$ (links) & \tier{form\_exact} & \tier{form\_exact} & invariant; also gauge-dependent \\
$\max|\|U\|-1|$ & \tier{form\_exact} & \tier{form\_exact} & invariant \\
mean $\operatorname{Re}P$ (faces) & $z=6.6$ \tier{function} & $z=2.0$ & \\
Wilson action at $\beta=1$ & $z=6.6$ & $z=2.0$ & affine to the above \\
loop trace & $z=1.1$ & $z=0.8$ & \tier{no\_power} \\
order sensitivity & $z=0.6$ & $z=0.2$ & \tier{no\_power} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Six witnesses carry at most three independent readings. Two are exactly invariant
(Prop.~\ref{prop:degenerate}). The Wilson action at $\beta=1$ equals
$\textit{wh}\,(1-\langle\operatorname{Re}P\rangle)$, hence is affine to the face mean, and
their $z$ agree to nine decimals because they must. Two have $C(W)<\tau$
(Prop.~\ref{prop:ceiling}). All four collapses are structural rather than statistical.

\begin{remark}
A corollary worth recording. The link mean has no consumer anywhere in the implementing
codebase, and the instrument supplies the reason: it is gauge-\emph{dependent} --- a gauge
transformation moves it while the face mean holds to nine decimals --- and
surrogate-invariant. Gauge-dependent together with invariant exhausts the ways a lattice
witness can fail to inform.
\end{remark}

That the same three propositions bite on two corpora sharing no subject matter is the
evidence that they are amendments to the method rather than to one dataset.

\section{A removable artifact in the surrogate construction}\label{sec:artifact}

FF06e attributes its $0.4$--$1.0\sigma$ form-witness band to its surrogate path: permute
unfolded spacings, re-accumulate, then interpolate back onto the reference grid. The
attribution is correct as to mechanism and localises further than stated. Two routes from
unfolded positions back to heights:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Route & spacing mean dev. & spacing variance & Wigner $L^2$ \\
\midrule
Analytic: bisect the monotone $\langle N\rangle$ & $6.2\times10^{-16}$ & $4.4\times10^{-15}$ & exactly $0$ \\
Piecewise-linear between samples & $1.9\times10^{-16}$ & $\mathbf{1.03\times10^{-5}}$ & $\mathbf{3.51\times10^{-4}}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The artifact requires interpolation between knots, not the round trip. Inverting the
smooth counting function by bisection restores exact invariance. A caveat a better
pipeline removes should be removed rather than documented.

We do not compare the resulting $z$ against FF06e's $0.4$--$1.0$: ours are $0.16$ and
$0.01$, and $z$ here is a ratio of one small shift to another, dominated by grid, $N$ and
interpolation detail. The finding is $\sigma_W$ moving from machine epsilon to
$10^{-5}$; its standardisation is not a quantity worth comparing across implementations.

\section{Discussion}

The amendments cost something, and the cost is worth naming. Prop.~\ref{prop:ceiling}
converts some former \emph{form} verdicts into \emph{unresolved}, which is a loss of
apparent information. The trade is favourable for an asymmetric reason: an unresolved
verdict can be improved by a better witness or a larger object, whereas a false
\emph{form} verdict cannot be improved, because it does not know it is wrong.

Prop.~\ref{prop:degenerate} replaces a number with a refusal. This is the amendment most
likely to be resisted, since a table with an omission looks weaker than a table of
figures. It is the other way around: the figure was never a measurement, and a reader
who takes $1.0\sigma$ for a small real effect has been misled by the presentation rather
than by the instrument.

Read as descent (\S\ref{sec:descent}), the three propositions say something compact. The
surrogate construction is a choice of quotient. A witness either factors through that
quotient or it does not; if it does, there is nothing to test, and if it does not, whether
it \emph{detects} the fibre is a separate question of power and of the particular object.
Conflating ``does not factor'' with ``detects'' is the single error behind all three
omissions.

\section{Scope and claim boundaries}\label{sec:scope}

\textbf{What a \tier{function} verdict establishes.} That the witness responds to
structure beyond the object's one-point distribution, relative to that object and that
surrogate construction. It does \emph{not} establish that the witness is correct,
meaningful, or evidence for any proposition about the object. FF06e is explicit on this
and it bears repeating, because a large $z$ reads as a result: a witness may respond
strongly to the object and be the wrong statistic.

\textbf{No claim is made about $\zeta$.} Nothing here concerns the Riemann hypothesis, the
construction of a self-adjoint operator, or off-line behaviour. Corpus I is used as an
object with known structure on which to measure a method.

\textbf{No claim is made about $SU(2)$ gauge theory.} Corpus II is a finite lattice
configuration used for the same purpose.

\textbf{Where the method stops.} It requires a surrogate preserving the marginal while
destroying correlation. Where no such construction exists --- where the object cannot be
quotiented to separate its response from the instrument's --- the form/function question
is well posed and not answerable by this operator. That is the tool's edge, not a defect
in results obtained inside it.

\textbf{Implementation dependence.} \S\ref{sec:artifact} now rests on two independent
implementations of each route (23 October 2026). Both find machine epsilon under exact
inversion against roughly $10^{-5}$ under piecewise-linear interpolation; neither
reproduces FF06e's band digits (the second gives median $z$ of $0.21$ and $0.09$ over
twenty seeds). The mechanism is robust to implementation; the particular figures are not.

\appendix
\section{Dead ends and self-corrections}

Recorded because the method's own discipline requires it, and because two of the results
above exist only as the residue of an error.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{The ascent count was predicted to read \tier{function}.} Asserted in
calibration; the assertion failed and the verdict was correct. This became
Prop.~\ref{prop:necessary}, the amendment with the widest reach.

\item \textbf{An analytic round trip was expected to reproduce FF06e's band.} It does not
--- $\sigma_W$ stays at machine epsilon. The negative result showed
``re-interpolation'' to be too coarse a diagnosis and produced \S\ref{sec:artifact}.

\item \textbf{Two implementations of a Condorcet cyclicity figure disagreed by
$3.4\sigma$} ($49.2\%$ against $46.5\%$ at five candidates, $4000$ trials each) --- a
spread as large as a discrepancy then being claimed against a third party's published
number. The claim was withdrawn until two seeds at $20\,000$ trials and three classical
anchors settled it. The disagreement is retained as the apparatus band it was.

\item \textbf{A mean was read as a partial collapse.} On the two-phase lattice
configuration the face mean is $0.263775$, which is the exact count-weighted average of
$28$ faces at $0.05342509$ and $8$ at exactly $1$, and is attained by no face. The
surrogate test then shows the mean sits $\approx2\sigma$ from its own null on that
object: the domain structure is invisible to it and was visible only in the histogram.
A scalar that cannot see what its own relation shows.
\end{enumerate}
