# Finite spectral information beyond shared field invariants

The discriminant-125 benchmark is already identified by its global arithmetic inputs. This checkpoint supplies a different benchmark where those inputs leave two actual fields possible, and a certified finite spectral statistic separates them. Let A = Q(sqrt(2), sqrt(-3)) and B = Q(sqrt(-2), sqrt(-3)). Both have degree four, signature (0,2), field discriminant 576 and Galois group V4. The coefficient c_7 in -zeta_K'/zeta_K = sum c_n log(p) n^(-s), with n = p^k, is 4 for A and 0 for B. These are nonisomorphic fields, not merely feasible vectors in a local relaxation.

The [checkpoint](../../docs/frontier/2026-09-13-biquadratic-delta/README.md) preserves arithmetic certificates, two complete finite spectra, independent audits, unsuccessful replay and corrected replay, sources, receipts and the continuing research queue. No global completion or exhaustion is claimed.

For a = 2 or -2, write beta^2 = a and alpha = (1 + sqrt(-3))/2, so alpha^2 = alpha - 1. The integral order with basis 1, alpha, beta, alpha beta has trace-Gram discriminant 576. If a larger integral order exists, the index has a prime divisor p in {2,3}. Its quotient contains an element of order p, represented by a nonzero vector (c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)/p modulo this basis. There are 15 such vectors for p = 2 and 80 for p = 3. For each, the characteristic polynomial of multiplication has a nonintegral coefficient, excluding integrality. All 190 exclusions across the two fields are independently replayed with Newton identities in the radical basis 1, sqrt(-3), sqrt(a), sqrt(-3a). Thus 576 is the field discriminant.

A separate classical route gives the same maximal basis: the quadratic fields have discriminants 4a and -3, which are coprime; they are distinct Galois quadratic fields, so their intersection is Q. Their product integral basis is maximal by [Baker, Proposition 2.42, printed page 52](https://www.math.toronto.edu/~ila/ANTBook.pdf#page=58). The three quadratic subfields have discriminants {8,-3,-24} for A and {-8,-3,24} for B. These sets differ, proving nonisomorphism of the Galois fields. Squares modulo seven independently distinguish their splitting.

The primitive element theta = alpha + beta has polynomial x^4 - 2x^3 - x^2 + 2x + 7 for A and x^4 - 2x^3 + 7x^2 - 6x + 3 for B. Their polynomial discriminants are 69696 and 14400, and their power-basis indices are 11 and 5. Factoring those polynomials at their index primes would falsely suggest field ramification. The independent audit switches to alpha + 2 beta at these primes. It verifies irreducibility and multiplication back for 144 local polynomial factorizations, covering both fields at all 72 primes through 361. At 2 both fields have (e,f,g) = (2,2,1); at 3 the models are (2,2,1) for A and (2,1,2) for B. Nineteen of the 72 local four-coefficient observables differ.

The candidate class is exhaustive under the stated V4 premise. A quadratic subfield can ramify only at 2 and 3, so its squarefree radicand lies in {-6,-3,-2,-1,2,3,6}. Exactly seven triples are closed under multiplication of square classes. The abelian conductor-discriminant formula gives each quartic discriminant as the product of its three quadratic character conductors. Exactly two triples have discriminant 576 and signature (0,2), namely A and B. An independent enumeration of the seven two-dimensional subspaces in the character group of (Z/24Z)^* agrees, computing conductors by descent to residue classes. The classical premise is [Milne, Class Field Theory, V.3.27 and Example 3.29, printed page 165](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=174). This does not enumerate non-V4 quartics. The earlier frozen pair producer only claimed two examples; the later class certificate strengthens that statement without modifying the producer.

The common input contract includes degree, signature, field discriminant, V4, the ramified-prime valuations 2^6 times 3^2 and the nontrivial conductor multiset {3,8,24}. Quadratic-subfield identities, defining polynomials, factor-labelled zeros, Euler coefficients and the conductor-parity assignment of the observed field are withheld. The last distinction matters: A has conductor/parity pairs (3,odd),(8,even),(24,odd), while B has (3,odd),(8,odd),(24,even). Those assignments would disclose additional information. The common degree, signature and discriminant supply identical Dedekind gamma and conductor terms.

The factorization is zeta_A = zeta L_8 L_-3 L_-24 and zeta_B = zeta L_-8 L_-3 L_24, where L_D uses the primitive Kronecker character of fundamental discriminant D. This identity is not inferred from a finite coefficient match. At an unramified prime, a Frobenius element in V4 has order one or two. Its values under the four characters yield respectively inverse Euler polynomials (1-T)^4 or (1-T^2)^2, exactly the field splitting polynomials. At the only ramified primes 2 and 3, the independently checked decomposition models give the same polynomials as the character products. Equality follows for every Euler factor, then by analytic continuation. In particular c_(p^k) = 1 + sum_D chi_D(p)^k. The finite 1,728-entry arithmetic vector and the 144 polynomial factorizations check implementations of this general identity.

All character residues, conductors, primitivity, order and reality are verified against exact Kronecker symbols. The five factors for D = -3, 8, -8, 24, -24 have respectively 4, 7, 7, 10, 11 positive zeros through height 20. Riemann zeta contributes one. The first zeros are approximately 1.977190514437953 and 2.688658132467598. Certified inequalities give gamma_A,1 < 2, gamma_B,1 > 8/3 and gamma_A,2 > 8/3, with both first roots above 1/3. No unknown zero above 20 can enter this gate under that error bound.

At displacement radius 1/2, both fields can produce either count 0 or count 1 at 7/3: uniform shifts by plus or minus 1/2 witness both outcomes. Thus this particular count is ambiguous. No impossibility result for full noisy spectra follows. The supremal radius for separating just the first-zero pair by one threshold is (gamma_B,1 - gamma_A,1)/2, approximately 0.3557338090148223, with optimal threshold approximately 2.3329243234527755. Exact rational enclosures are retained. This is not an optimum over every count gate or every spectral statistic.
