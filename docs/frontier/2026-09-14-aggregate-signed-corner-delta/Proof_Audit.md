# Audit of the signed corner implication

This is a written mathematical audit by the implementing investigator, not a separate human or agent review. The independently implemented numerical audit verifies the analytic sign premises and corner values using different formulas. Both routes share FLINT and inherited source data. Generic Lean proofs cover the algebra stated in the proof guide; the assembled analytic theorem is not wholly formalized.

1. **Pointwise to finite differences.** A pointwise inverse maximum alone would be insufficient. For example, F(x,y)=(exp(x)cos(y),exp(x)sin(y)) on the convex rectangle [0,1] x [0,2*pi] has determinant exp(2*x)>0 and a bounded inverse derivative, yet F(0,0)=F(0,2*pi). Its second-column secant between these points is zero. The new ACS argument instead constructs each secant column as an integral in a closed convex hull and proves that every matrix in the resulting product family is nonsingular. The failure of the shortcut does not undermine that stronger hypothesis.

2. **Closure and correlated entries.** The derivative curve is vector valued. A column integral lies in its closed convex hull by limits of Riemann sums. Affine coordinate inequalities extend to that hull. Entrywise interval mixtures would give a larger family and are not used for endpoint optimization. For any arbitrary old column in the closed hull, the endpoint comparison uses a continuous linear functional, so convex combinations and their limits preserve it.

3. **Uniform nonsingularity and determinant sign.** The bound |I-RM|<=B holds on the full hull family, with nonnegative B and strictly positive w satisfying Bw<w/2. The Neumann series excludes singularity there. The family is convex, hence connected, and a continuous nonzero determinant has constant sign. Merely checking corner or endpoint determinants would not suffice: I and -I in dimension two both have determinant +1, while their midpoint is singular. The exact control retains this counterexample.

4. **Positive resolvent and sign penalty.** Exact residuals identify the critical row of (I-B)^-1. The certificate explicitly checks its row minus the identity is nonnegative. Both h identities and the independent supersolution inequality are verified. All 22 critical inverse-row signs are stable only at radius .01. The radius-.03 result includes a nonzero penalty for four unresolved signs. Omitting this penalty would be unsupported.

5. **Curvature uniformity.** The factor e1^T M^-1 g''(t) is bounded uniformly with M and t varied independently. Taylor coefficients retain cancellation after multiplication by R; the sixth-order remainder uses absolute values and the eighth scalar derivative divided by 6!, across the entire coordinate interval. The independent real-polynomial route reproduces the signs with its own previously certified majorant. The exact polynomial t^8 control detects the wrong-factorial underestimate.

6. **Finite replacement orientation.** For A new and B old, the exact difference is -A^-1(A-B)B^-1. With a single column change, the factor at the right is (B^-1 v)_j. Reusing the new inverse there generally produces only a differential approximation. An exact two-by-two control distinguishes the formulas. The compiled column-action and replacement lemmas verify the finite algebra under explicit inverse hypotheses.

7. **Endpoint comparison.** Hold the new matrix A fixed while integrating the scalar derivative e1^T A^-1 g''(t). Its sign determines the linear functional's endpoint extremum on the whole column hull. Choose the endpoint direction opposite the product of curvature sign and old inverse-direction sign. Then the inverse identity gives a nonnegative change of K. This step holds for mixed columns, so iterating 21 replacements is valid. It is not an argument restricted to point Jacobians.

8. **Exceptional coordinate 12.** Its global inverse-direction enclosure crosses zero. No global sign is claimed. Only after the other 21 columns are fixed does the Cramer numerator become a fixed nonzero scalar independent of column 12. A negative face-reference quotient and the already established determinant orientation give a negative quotient on the entire face. The compiled numerator-overwrite lemma and quotient-sign lemma support this step. Replacing the wrong column in the numerator would fail; the exact control demonstrates its dependence.

9. **Corner and threshold scope.** The final corner Jacobian is itself in the matrix family, so the written replacement proof gives an attained maximum of its critical inverse-row norm on that outer family. Independent determinant ratios verify the primary dyadic upper bound. This yields a bound on every observation secant. It does not make the corner a source-feasible pair, prove the sharp source-constrained threshold, settle equality at the lower endpoint, supply arbitrary-report decoding or extend the physical precision contract.

10. **Arithmetic consequence.** Field separation follows from a critical source gap of at least 2(L-r), the uniform secant constant, and the triangle inequality for a common report. Independent errors strictly below (L-r)/K cannot hide field identity within the complete inherited class. The 604 tracked coefficients and 456/148 ambiguity split at inherited ambiguous reports are independently rechecked by polynomial arithmetic. Field classification and source-population premises remain inherited and recursively verified.

The unresolved next questions are the source-feasible threshold gap, useful source-constrained refinements, improved actual pairs, practical bounded-error decoding, larger/global domains and complete analytic formalization. All other ACS branches remain active.
