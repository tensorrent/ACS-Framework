# Hidden-coupling closure test — prospective protocol

Date: 2026-09-24. This extends the sealed canonical-vacuum audit without changing it.

Question: which of the six quartics invisible on the neutral vacuum can consistently
be omitted, and can the inherited ACS constraints determine the remaining coefficients?

Use the same 68 real fields, kinetic metric, and ordered 17-invariant basis. In this
document indices are zero-based. The hidden indices are 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12. All beta
coefficients below use B = 32 pi^2 d/d log(mu). The calculation is in the unbroken
minimal theory in MS; it is not a broken-phase threshold or a pole-mass calculation.

Tests fixed before new calculations:

1. Recover the archived full one-loop tables with hashes and explicit archive paths.
   Rebuild all six gauge-quartic polynomials directly from the 21 infinitesimal gauge
   actions and metric. Require exact equality on every monomial, not a fitted sample.
2. Check the complete beta polynomial for each holomorphic coefficient on the surface
   lambda11=lambda12=0, including scalar, gauge, and arbitrary-flavor Yukawa terms.
   Identify the charge selection rule and keep its anomaly qualification explicit.
3. Independently compare the assembled 17-quartic beta potential against the canonical
   Hessian, vector Gram, fermion mass map, and wave terms on generic fields and couplings
   at one, two, and three families. Require relative residual below 1e-9, normalized by
   the sum of absolute component contributions. These are implementation checks.
4. Use the gauge-free isotropic scalar model as a preservation control. Test the same
   scalar ansatz with gauge interactions and a Majorana Yukawa interaction separately.
   Unequal derivatives of the five Delta norm coefficients falsify isotropic closure.
5. Resolve the old ambiguous trace notation using explicit color-Gram and spin-Gram
   contractions. Both must be tested off the neutral rank-one slice. Do not assign one
   to the historical rho2 without an explicit source definition.
6. Integrate a small-coupling illustrative one-loop scalar/gauge trajectory from an
   isotropic boundary, with zero Yukawas, forward and backward in log scale. Compare
   two tolerances and the initial derivative. This demonstrates closure or its failure;
   it does not identify ACS boundary data or a physical scale.

Preserve failures and attempts. Add new artifacts only. Record what is derived,
symmetry-protected, conditionally initialized, and still undetermined. Running is not
a replacement for boundary conditions; no RH, matter-origin, or physical-mass proof is
implied. Prior evidence receipts must remain unchanged.
