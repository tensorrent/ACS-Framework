\begin{abstract}
The M\"obius-screw note models the electron as a framed unknot with
self-linking number $Sl=2$ under the torus framing, and identifies
$Sl \leftrightarrow g$ as a bookkeeping statement.  This note computes
the full chain of maps from the centerline to the $SU(2)$ lift of its
frame field, and evaluates every stage.  Three results.
\emph{(i)} The C\u{a}lug\u{a}reanu decomposition is confirmed
numerically: $Tw = -1.0338$, $Wr = -0.9662$, $Tw+Wr = -2.000000$, and the
Gauss linking integral of the centerline with its framing pushoff returns
$-2.000000$; the magnitude $|Sl| = pq = 2$ is as stated, the sign records
that the embedding as parameterised is left-handed.
\emph{(ii)} The frame loop is genuinely \emph{spinorial}: its class in
$\pi_1(SO(3)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ is non-trivial, so the $SU(2)$ lift
does not close after one circuit.  This is proved in closed form, not
merely computed.
\emph{(iii)} The spin content is carried by a \emph{parity bit}, not by
the value $2$.  For torus curves the holonomy is
$\sigma = (-1)^{p+q} = (-1)^{Sl+1}$, so $Sl = 2$ and $Sl = 0$ lie in the
same class: an untwisted round circle is spinorial in exactly the same
sense.  The magnitude of $Sl$ carries no spin information, which
withdraws the support the $Sl=2 \leftrightarrow g=2$ identification was
taken to have.  What survives is sharper and smaller: the double cover in
this model comes from the meridian half-angle $\varphi/2$ in the
parameterisation, i.e.\ from the \emph{odd} meridian winding $q=1$.
The parity law itself is not new --- it is standard in the framed-curve
and Kirby-calculus literature (\S\ref{sec:lit}) --- and the contribution
here is its evaluation on this curve, together with the resulting
correction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}

This note is a computation, not a new model.  It takes the centerline and
framing already defined in the M\"obius-screw note and asks a single
question: \emph{at which stage, if any, does spin-$1/2$ enter?}  Every
number below is produced by
\texttt{code/framed\_unknot/framing\_transformer.py} and archived in
\texttt{docs/framed\_unknot\_results.json}.

Nothing here bears on QED, on radiative corrections to $g-2$, or on the
capacitance estimate of $\alpha$.  The result is topological.

\section{The transformer chain}

The object is the closed curve, $\varphi \in [0,4\pi)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:curve}
\bm{\gamma}(\varphi) =
\begin{pmatrix}
\bigl(R + a\cos(\varphi/2)\bigr)\cos\varphi \\
\bigl(R + a\cos(\varphi/2)\bigr)\sin\varphi \\
a\sin(\varphi/2)
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
which is the $(p,q) = (2,1)$ curve on the torus of radii $(R,a)$:
longitude angle $\theta = \varphi$, meridian angle $\psi = \varphi/2$.
The torus surface normal
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:framing}
\bm{U}(\varphi) = \bigl(\cos(\varphi/2)\cos\varphi,\;
                        \cos(\varphi/2)\sin\varphi,\;
                        \sin(\varphi/2)\bigr)
\end{equation}
is the natural framing.  Because $\bm{\gamma}$ lies in the surface whose
normal is $\bm{U}$, orthogonality $\bm{T}\cdot\bm{U} = 0$ holds
identically (verified to $2.2\times10^{-16}$).

The chain from geometry to spin is
\[
\bm{\gamma}
\;\xrightarrow{\ \text{framing}\ }\;
\bm{U}
\;\xrightarrow{\ \text{CWF}\ }\;
Sl = Tw + Wr
\;\xrightarrow{\ \text{adapted frame}\ }\;
F : S^1 \to SO(3)
\;\xrightarrow{\ \text{lift}\ }\;
q : S^1 \to SU(2),
\]
with $F(\varphi) = [\bm{T},\bm{U},\bm{T}\times\bm{U}]$.  The last arrow is
the only stage at which a double cover can appear, and whether it
succeeds is decided by the single sign
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma}
\sigma \;=\; \frac{q(4\pi)}{q(0)} \in \{+1,-1\},
\end{equation}
$\sigma = +1$ meaning the lift closes (frame returns after one circuit)
and $\sigma = -1$ meaning it does not (two circuits required).

\section{Stage 1: the C\u{a}lug\u{a}reanu decomposition}

Twist is evaluated as
$Tw = \tfrac{1}{2\pi}\oint (\bm{T}\times\bm{U})\cdot \bm{U}'\,d\varphi$,
writhe by the Gauss double integral, and $Sl$ independently as the linking
number $\mathrm{Lk}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\gamma}+\epsilon\bm{U})$.

Quantity & Value & Method \\
\midrule

$Wr$                 & $-0.966239$ & Gauss double integral \\
$Tw + Wr$            & $-2.000000$ & C\u{a}lug\u{a}reanu sum \\
$\mathrm{Lk}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\gamma}+0.10a\bm{U})$ & $-2.000000$ & Gauss linking integral \\

The magnitude $|Sl| = 2$ claimed in the M\"obius-screw note is confirmed
by two independent routes.  The negative sign is not an error: it records
that the embedding \eqref{eq:curve}, with meridian angle advancing as
$+\varphi/2$, is left-handed under the standard Gauss convention.  Only
$|Sl|$ and its parity are used below.

\begin{remark}[What is and is not topological here]
$Tw$ and $Wr$ individually depend on $R$ and $a$ and are not invariants;
only their sum is.  The near-equal split
$(-1.03, -0.97)$ at $a/R = 0.3$ is a coincidence of that aspect ratio, not
a structural fact, and should not be read as ``one unit of twist plus one
unit of writhe.''
\end{remark}

\section{Stage 2: the $SU(2)$ lift, in closed form}

The framing \eqref{eq:framing} is exactly the sphere parameterisation at
longitude $\varphi$ and latitude $\varphi/2$, so
$\bm{U}(\varphi) = A(\varphi)\,\bm{e}_x$ with
\begin{equation}
A(\varphi) = R_z(\varphi)\, R_y(-\varphi/2).
\end{equation}
Lifting each factor to unit quaternions
($R_z(t)\mapsto \cos\tfrac{t}{2} + k\sin\tfrac{t}{2}$,
 $R_y(t)\mapsto \cos\tfrac{t}{2} + j\sin\tfrac{t}{2}$) gives the
continuous lift with $q(0)=1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lift}
q(\varphi) =
\Bigl[\cos\tfrac{\varphi}{2} + k\,\sin\tfrac{\varphi}{2}\Bigr]
\Bigl[\cos\tfrac{\varphi}{4} - j\,\sin\tfrac{\varphi}{4}\Bigr].
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Spinorial holonomy]
\label{prop:spinorial}
For the curve \eqref{eq:curve} with framing \eqref{eq:framing},
\[
q(4\pi) = \bigl[\cos 2\pi + k \sin 2\pi\bigr]\,
          \bigl[\cos \pi - j \sin \pi\bigr]
        = (1)(-1) = -1 ,
\]
so $\sigma = -1$: the frame loop represents the non-trivial class of
$\pi_1(SO(3)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ and its $SU(2)$ lift does not close
after one circuit of the curve.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Where the sign comes from]
The meridian factor.  Over the closed curve the meridian angle advances
by $2\pi$, so its half-angle in \eqref{eq:lift} advances by only $\pi$,
landing on $-1$.  The longitude factor advances by $4\pi$, half-angle
$2\pi$, landing on $+1$ and contributing nothing.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:spinorial} is confirmed numerically three ways, all
returning $\sigma = -1.000000$: the adapted frame $[\bm{T},\bm{U},
\bm{T}\times\bm{U}]$ lifted by continuity, the framing field $A(\varphi)$
lifted by continuity, and direct evaluation of \eqref{eq:lift}.

\section{Stage 3: the value $2$ does no work}

Generalising Proposition~\ref{prop:spinorial} to the $(p,q)$ torus curve
with torus framing: the longitude half-angle advances by $\pi p$ and the
meridian half-angle by $\pi q$, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:parity}
\sigma = (-1)^{p+q}.
\end{equation}
Since $Sl = pq$ and $\gcd(p,q)=1$ forbids both being even, $p+q$ is odd
exactly when $pq$ is even, hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:parity2}
\sigma = (-1)^{Sl+1}.
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Convention, stated so it cannot be misread]
\label{rem:convention}
Equation~\eqref{eq:parity2} is normalised to the adapted frame
$[\bm{T},\bm{U},\bm{T}\times\bm{U}]$ with $Sl$ measured against the
Seifert ($0$-) framing.  In that normalisation the round unknot with the
$0$-framing is the \emph{generator} of $\pi_1(SO(3))$ --- it does not
lift --- because the tangent indicatrix itself contributes one turn.
Absolute parity statements are therefore convention-dependent and easy to
get backwards.  The convention-free form, which is what should be quoted,
is:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Incrementing the framing by $1$ flips the $\pi_1(SO(3))$ class;
framings differing by $2$ lie in the same component.}
\end{quote}
This is the belt trick, and it is the statement that survives any choice
of reference framing.  Compare Needham~\cite{needham2017}, Thm.~3.7,
where an $|h|$-fold covered circle of self-linking $k$ has closed
quaternion lift iff $h+k$ is even --- which for $h=1$ reproduces the
control column below exactly.
\end{remark}

This was checked against an independent control family --- round circles
carrying $n$ framing twists, for which $Wr = 0$ and $|Sl| = n$ --- with
the measured result reproducing \eqref{eq:parity2} exactly:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrl@{}}
\toprule
$n$ & $Tw$ & $Wr$ & $Sl$ & class \\
\midrule
$-2$ & $+2$ & $0$ & $+2$ & spinorial \\
$-1$ & $+1$ & $0$ & $+1$ & trivial \\
$0$  & $0$  & $0$ & $0$  & spinorial \\
$1$  & $-1$ & $0$ & $-1$ & trivial \\
$2$  & $-2$ & $0$ & $-2$ & spinorial \\
$3$  & $-3$ & $0$ & $-3$ & trivial \\
$4$  & $-4$ & $0$ & $-4$ & spinorial \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{corollary}[The identification $Sl=2 \leftrightarrow g=2$ loses its support]
\label{cor:kill}
The spin-relevant content of the self-linking number is one bit, its
parity.  A framed loop with $Sl = 0$ --- an ordinary round circle with an
untwisted framing --- is spinorial in precisely the same sense as the
M\"obius screw with $|Sl| = 2$.  The \emph{value} $2$ therefore carries no
spin information that $0$ does not also carry, and cannot be what
produces the double cover.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Status change]
The M\"obius-screw note (\S3.3) writes that $Sl = 2$ ``matches the $4\pi$
(two-turn) return of a spin-$1/2$ frame,'' and labels this a model
identification rather than a derivation.  Corollary~\ref{cor:kill}
removes the geometric motivation for the identification itself: the
matching quantity is a $\mathbb{Z}/2$ class, and $2$ and $0$ are the same
class.  Under the repository's tier discipline this is a
\textbf{T4 (explicitly falsified)} outcome for the reading of $Sl = 2$ as
the geometric origin of $g = 2$, and should be recorded in the
Elimination Ledger.  The rest of the M\"obius-screw note is untouched:
the geometry, the winding numbers, and the $|Sl| = 2$ computation are all
confirmed.
\end{remark}

\section{What survives, and it is worth keeping}

Something real remains, and it is more specific than what was claimed.
The double cover in this model is produced by the \textbf{meridian
half-angle} $\varphi/2$ appearing in \eqref{eq:curve} and
\eqref{eq:framing} --- that is, by the \emph{odd meridian winding}
$q = 1$, via \eqref{eq:parity}.  The screw's defining feature, that the
frame must traverse the curve twice to return, is a genuine topological
property of the shape, proved in Proposition~\ref{prop:spinorial}, and it
is exactly the $720^\circ$ property of a spin-$1/2$ frame.

So the model does land on the spinorial side of a genuine $\mathbb{Z}/2$
invariant.  That is a legitimate structural result and it is what the
tornado/Archimedean-screw picture was reaching for.  It is also
considerably weaker than a derivation of $g$: a $\mathbb{Z}/2$ bit cannot
produce a real number, and any model claiming a magnitude from this
structure needs a mechanism that \eqref{eq:parity} does not supply.

\subsection{The tornado picture is the same object, not a metaphor}
\label{sec:throat}

Appendix~A of the M\"obius-screw note offers a ``tornado'' reading --- a
funnel narrowing to a high-torsion throat around a quiet axis --- and
labels it interpretive, adding no formal claims.  That labelling is more
conservative than it needs to be.  Sweeping the aspect ratio $a/R$
towards $1$ carries the curve continuously from a fat-hole torus to
exactly that funnel: since $\rho(\varphi) = R + a\cos(\varphi/2)$, the
closest approach to the axis is $\rho_{\min} = R - a$, attained at
$\varphi = 2\pi$, and it shrinks to zero as $a \to R$.  The throat is not
an added feature of the picture; it is where the centerline meets the
axis.

Across that entire sweep the invariants do not move:

\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$a/R$ & $\rho_{\min}$ & $Tw$ & $Wr$ & $\sigma$ \\
\midrule
$0.30$ & $0.700$ & $-1.03376$ & $-0.96624$ & $-1$ \\
$0.50$ & $0.500$ & $-1.09066$ & $-0.90934$ & $-1$ \\
$0.70$ & $0.300$ & $-1.14910$ & $-0.85090$ & $-1$ \\
$0.85$ & $0.150$ & $-1.15375$ & $-0.84625$ & $-1$ \\
$0.97$ & $0.030$ & $-1.11872$ & $-0.88128$ & $-1$ \\
$0.999$& $0.001$ & $-1.10597$ & $-0.89403$ & $-1$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

$Tw + Wr = -2.000000$ at every row, and the holonomy stays $\sigma = -1$
throughout.  This is what isotopy invariance looks like when you watch it:
the deformation is continuous and embedded for all $a < R$, degenerating
only in the limit, so donut and vortex are one framed loop and the
topology cannot distinguish them.  Note also that $Tw$ is
\emph{non-monotonic} across the sweep, turning over near $a/R \approx
0.85$ --- further evidence that the individual terms are geometry with no
invariant meaning.

The consequence cuts both ways.  The tornado picture is licensed as a
faithful drawing of the formal object, which it was not previously
claimed to be.  But it inherits the object's limits exactly: the throat
is not an extra structure that could supply a magnitude, because it is
reachable by deformation from a shape that has no throat at all.

\section{The successor test, run: geometry gives $g=1$}
\label{sec:moment}

The obvious next step is not a larger analogy but a smaller question:
compute the shape's \emph{magnetic moment} against its \emph{angular
momentum} and read off a $g$ that has dimensions and can fail.  We ran it
(\texttt{code/framed\_unknot/moment\_ratio.py}).  It fails, and the way it
fails is instructive.

Let a particle of charge $q$ and mass $m$ traverse the closed centerline
once per period $T$, and write the vector area
\begin{equation}
\bm{A} = \tfrac{1}{2}\oint \bm{r}\times d\bm{l}.
\end{equation}
Then the current is $I = q/T$, and
\begin{equation}
\bm{\mu} = I\bm{A} = \frac{q}{T}\bm{A},
\qquad
\langle \bm{L}\rangle = \frac{m}{T}\oint \bm{r}\times d\bm{r}
                      = \frac{2m}{T}\bm{A}.
\end{equation}
Both are proportional to the \emph{same} geometric factor, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:g1}
\frac{\bm{\mu}}{\langle\bm{L}\rangle} = \frac{q}{2m}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
g = 1 \quad\text{exactly, for every closed curve.}
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Curve & $A_z$ & $A_z/\pi$ \\
\midrule
M\"obius screw $(2,1)$, $a/R = 0.30$ & $+6.56593$ & $+2.09000$ \\
M\"obius screw $(2,1)$, $a/R = 0.70$ & $+7.82257$ & $+2.49000$ \\
M\"obius screw $(2,1)$, $a/R = 0.97$ & $+9.23911$ & $+2.94090$ \\
round circle, one turn               & $+3.14159$ & $+1.00000$ \\
round circle, two turns              & $+6.28319$ & $+2.00000$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The double winding is entirely real: the screw carries $2.09\times$ the
vector area of a single loop.  It is also useless for $g$, because that
factor enters $\bm{\mu}$ and $\langle\bm{L}\rangle$ identically and
cancels --- the same way the $2$ in $Sl$ cancelled down to a parity bit in
\S5.  Note too that $A_z$ is \emph{not} an invariant: it runs from
$2.09\pi$ to $2.94\pi$ across the throat sweep of \S\ref{sec:throat},
unlike $Sl$, which does not move at all.

\begin{corollary}[No-go for geometric $g$]
\label{cor:nogo}
No model in which charge and mass circulate together with a uniform
$q/m$ ratio can produce $g \neq 1$ by geometry, whatever the winding,
framing, twist, or throat.  Equation~\eqref{eq:g1} is the classical
orbital $g$-factor, and it is shape-independent.  Obtaining $g \neq 1$
requires \emph{decoupling the charge and mass distributions} --- a
specific structural requirement, and a far better-posed target than
looking for a $2$ in the geometry.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{On the dynamo reading}
\label{sec:dynamo}

A natural physical reading of this shape is a dynamo, and one part of
that instinct is exactly right --- it is a theorem, not an analogy.  For
a thin magnetic flux tube of flux $\Phi$, the magnetic helicity is
\begin{equation}
H = \int \bm{A}\cdot\bm{B}\,d^3x = \Phi^2\,(Tw + Wr) = \Phi^2 Sl,
\end{equation}
so the C\u{a}lug\u{a}reanu quantity computed in \S3 \emph{is} the magnetic
helicity of a flux tube with this geometry
(Moffatt~\cite{moffatt1969}; Moffatt--Ricca~\cite{moffattricca1992}).
Dynamo action is likewise about exactly the $Tw \leftrightarrow Wr$ trade
seen in \S3: the stretch--twist--fold mechanism converts writhe into
twist while doubling flux.  The bookkeeping is the same bookkeeping.

Two things block the reading as an electron model, and neither is
topological.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{A dynamo grows.}  It has a growth rate and amplifies
    field by consuming kinetic energy from a flow.  A stable particle is
    a stationary state --- no growth, no energy source, no dissipation.
    The dynamical class is wrong.
  \item \textbf{Helicity is the invariant we already have.}  Since
    $H = \Phi^2 Sl$, the dynamo framing supplies the same integer, in the
    same units of $\Phi^2$.  It adds physical content but no new
    magnitude, and Corollary~\ref{cor:nogo} still applies.
\end{enumerate}

The nearest object that \emph{is} stationary is the force-free or Taylor
state: by Woltjer's theorem, a plasma relaxing at fixed magnetic helicity
minimises energy at $\nabla\times\bm{B} = \lambda\bm{B}$, a
self-organised helicity-carrying equilibrium (a spheromak).  That is the
right dynamical class, and $\lambda$ carries dimensions of inverse
length, so unlike the parity bit it does set a scale.  It still does not
evade Corollary~\ref{cor:nogo} on its own --- a force-free state with
co-located charge and mass gives $g=1$ like everything else --- but it
poses the question in the only form that could survive: what, in this
geometry, separates where the charge sits from where the mass sits?

\section{One object, four axes --- and the Laplace eigenspace}
\label{sec:oneobject}

Three structural readings of this geometry turn out to be exactly right,
and together they relocate where spin-$1/2$ lives.  All are verified in
\texttt{code/framed\_unknot/one\_object.py}.

\subsection{$(2,1)$ is the $xy$-axis twisted}

The pair $(p,q) = (2,1)$ is not two independent windings.  It is two
Euler angles of a \emph{single} rotation, locked at a $2\!:\!1$ rate:
\begin{equation}
\bm{U}(\varphi) = A(\varphi)\,\bm{e}_x,
\qquad A(\varphi) = R_z(\varphi)\,R_y(-\varphi/2),
\end{equation}
so $p = 2$ is the azimuthal turn in the $xy$-plane and $q = 1$ is the
tilt.  Checked against the direct torus normal, the difference is
$0.000\times10^{0}$ --- not small, but identically zero.  This is why the
lift factorises the way it does in \eqref{eq:lift}, and why the sign is
carried entirely by the half-angle of the tilt.

\subsection{The framed curve is one quaternion curve}

A framed curve looks like $3+1$ pieces of data --- a centerline plus a
normal field --- only in the naive presentation.  Through the frame-Hopf
map it is a \emph{single} quaternion-valued curve $q(\varphi)$ on $S^3$,
from which the framing and the remaining frame leg are recovered by
conjugation alone:
\begin{equation}
\bm{U} = q\,\bm{e}_x\,\bar q, \qquad \bm{V} = q\,\bm{e}_z\,\bar q .
\end{equation}
Reconstructing from $q$ and nothing else reproduces the direct formulas
to $3.3\times10^{-16}$, with $|q| = 1$ to $2.2\times10^{-16}$.  Four
numbers, one object.  (This is Needham's construction,
\cite{needham2017}~\S2.)

\subsection{Laplace: where spin-$1/2$ actually enters}

The four quaternion coordinates are not an arbitrary basis.  Restricted
to $S^3 = SU(2)$ they are exactly the first nonzero eigenspace of the
Laplace--Beltrami operator:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{S^3}\, x_i = -3\, x_i,
\qquad \text{degeneracy } (k+1)^2 = 4 \text{ at } k=1,
\end{equation}
measured here as $-3.00000$ on each of the four axes (max error
$8\times10^{-7}$ by finite differences on $2\times10^4$ sampled points).
Under $SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R$ that eigenspace is the
$(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ representation.

\begin{remark}[The relocation]
This is the honest home of spin-$1/2$ in this picture, and it is not the
self-linking number.  Spin enters as a \emph{Laplace eigenspace on the
quaternion sphere} --- i.e.\ through representation theory, which is
precisely where L\'evy-Leblond~\cite{levyleblond1967} locates $g = 2$ as
well.  \S5 showed the linking number contributes one parity bit;
\S\ref{sec:moment} showed the geometry contributes $g = 1$.  What is left
carrying the spin structure is the rep theory of $S^3$, which was never a
geometric claim about the electron's shape.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The chain silently requires rationality}

Worth recording because nothing above states it: the whole transformer
presupposes a \emph{closed} curve, and a torus curve closes iff its
winding ratio $q/p$ is rational.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
Winding ratio & Closes after \\
\midrule
$1/2$ \ \ (2,1) & 2 turns \\
$2/3$ \ \ (3,2) & 3 turns \\
$3/5$ \ \ (5,3) & 5 turns \\
$1/\phi$ (golden) & never --- dense \\
$1/\sqrt{2}$ & never --- dense \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At irrational slope the curve never closes, fills the torus densely, and
has \emph{no} self-linking number and \emph{no} $\pi_1$ class at all.
There is a real tension here worth naming: KAM theory makes the noble
(most irrational, golden-ratio) windings the \emph{most} robust invariant
tori under perturbation, while topological quantisation --- everything in
this note --- requires the rational ones.  Maximal dynamical stability
and topological quantisation pull in opposite directions on a torus.  Any
model that wants both owes an account of which it is choosing.

\section{Relation to the established literature}
\label{sec:lit}

The results of \S\S3--5 are not new mathematics.  They are a computation
of known structure applied to this specific curve, and it is worth
recording exactly which parts are textbook, because that is what makes
the T4 verdict binding rather than merely our opinion.

\paragraph{The parity law is known.}
That the space of closed framed curves in $\mathbb{R}^3$ has exactly two
path components, distinguished by the parity of the self-linking number,
with the mechanism being the double cover $SU(2)\to SO(3)$, is stated
outright by Needham~\cite{needham2017}, \S2.2, in the framed-curve
setting.  The same fact appears in Kirby calculus as: a framing of a knot
induces a spin structure on it, \emph{even} framings giving the bounding
structure and \emph{odd} framings the non-bounding one
(Gompf--Stipsicz~\cite{gompfstipsicz1999}, \S5.6--5.7).  The explicit
quaternionic frame-Hopf map and the closed/anti-closed dichotomy used in
\S4 are Needham's \S2.  For the C\u{a}lug\u{a}reanu decomposition the
modern geometric reference is Dennis--Hannay~\cite{dennishannay2005}.
Our \eqref{eq:lift} and Proposition~\ref{prop:spinorial} are that
machinery evaluated on \eqref{eq:curve}; the value added is the specific
evaluation, not the framework.

\paragraph{Framing genuinely carries spin data --- at the level of a
$\mathbb{Z}/2$.}
This is real and load-bearing elsewhere in physics.  It is why
Chern--Simons theory needs Atiyah's $2$-framings~\cite{atiyah1990} or
$p_1$-structures rather than framings; the current careful treatment is
Freed--Teleman~\cite{freedteleman2026}.  It is also why the topological
route to \emph{half-integer angular momentum} works at all:
Finkelstein--Rubinstein~\cite{finkelsteinrubinstein1968} and, in the form
closest to the present note, Wilczek--Zee~\cite{wilczekzee1983}, where a
\emph{linking number} (the Hopf term) produces fractional spin and
statistics in $2{+}1$ dimensions.  So ``linking number $\to$ spin'' is a
real mechanism.  What it produces is a phase and a statistics sector, not
a gyromagnetic ratio.

\paragraph{The step to $g$ is made nowhere.}
A literature sweep for a peer-reviewed derivation of $g=2$ from a framed
loop, ribbon, or self-linking structure returns nothing in the mainstream
record.  Decisively in the other direction,
L\'evy-Leblond~\cite{levyleblond1967} showed that $g=2$ follows from
\emph{linearising the Schr\"odinger equation} --- it is a consequence of
the spinor representation, and is neither relativistic nor topological in
origin.  Any geometric account of $g$ has to beat that, and none does.

The nearby speculative literature should be cited, if at all, with its
standing attached.  Kelvin's vortex atoms are historical only.
Battey-Pratt \& Racey (1980) is published but effectively uncited outside
fringe work and should not be used as authority.  Schiller's strand model
appears in peer-reviewed venues but its substance is self-published with
no independent uptake.  Bilson-Thompson's braided preons are
peer-reviewed and have modest uptake within loop quantum gravity, but the
author states explicitly that the model does \emph{not} explain the
origin of spin --- ribbon twist there encodes electric charge.  Hestenes'
geometric-algebra reformulation of the Dirac equation is mathematically
sound; the physical zitterbewegung reading is a minority interpretation
and recovers $g=2$ from the Dirac equation, which already supplies it.

\paragraph{One trap worth naming.}
``Half-integer hopfion'' and ``fractional skyrmion'' in the 2024--2026
condensed-matter literature mean a half-integer \emph{topological index},
not half-integer \emph{angular momentum}.  These are not spin-$1/2$
objects and must not be cited as precedent for one.
